# English Curriculum

This curriculum is organized around real professional outcomes, not isolated grammar topics. Lessons should stay small, practical, and tied to the mission in `MISSION.md`.

## Phase 1 — Professional Communication Foundations

### Lesson 0001 — Softening and collaborative language
Goal: disagree, suggest, and negotiate without sounding rude.
Skills: `I think...`, `Maybe we could...`, `What if we...`, `I’m not sure...`, `Could...`, `Would...`.
Evidence of mastery: respond to a design or scope disagreement with a collaborative alternative.

### Lesson 0002 — Explaining value without sounding defensive
Goal: defend price and quality using concrete value.
Skills: acknowledge budget concerns, reframe price as value, describe deliverables, avoid vague claims like “my work is better”.
Evidence of mastery: answer “the price is too high” with a clear value explanation and one trade-off.

### Lesson 0003 — Negotiating scope, deadline, and revisions
Goal: change one variable without giving away everything.
Skills: scope-price-time trade-offs, conditional language, deadline extensions, revision limits.
Evidence of mastery: negotiate a smaller scope, a longer deadline, or fewer revisions in a realistic client scenario.

### Lesson 0004 — Clarifying and checking understanding in meetings
Goal: recover when speech is fast or unclear without freezing.
Skills: ask for repetition, paraphrase, confirm decisions, buy thinking time.
Evidence of mastery: handle a fast stakeholder explanation and confirm the next action.

## Phase 2 — Sales and Client Calls

### Lesson 0005 — Discovery calls
Goal: understand the client’s business problem before proposing a solution.
Skills: open questions, follow-up questions, summarizing needs, identifying constraints.
Evidence of mastery: run a short discovery conversation for a landing page or product redesign.

### Lesson 0006 — Presenting a premium offer
Goal: explain a landing page, redesign, or subscription offer in outcome-focused English.
Skills: positioning, deliverables, process, speed, quality, daily alignment, implementation.
Evidence of mastery: present the offer in 60–90 seconds without reading a script.

### Lesson 0007 — Handling price objections
Goal: respond to budget concerns calmly and strategically.
Skills: acknowledge, reframe, trade-off, close the next step.
Evidence of mastery: handle three different price objections with distinct responses.

### Lesson 0008 — Closing and next steps
Goal: move a conversation toward a decision without sounding pushy.
Skills: recap, decision language, next steps, timelines, payment terms.
Evidence of mastery: close a mock call with a clear action plan.

## Phase 3 — Product and UX English

### Lesson 0009 — Explaining design decisions
Goal: connect UI choices to user and business outcomes.
Skills: rationale, hierarchy, clarity, conversion, friction, usability.
Evidence of mastery: explain one real CalcMed or landing-page decision in English.

### Lesson 0010 — Giving and receiving design feedback
Goal: participate in critique without becoming defensive or vague.
Skills: specific feedback, alternatives, trade-offs, prioritization.
Evidence of mastery: respond to feedback and propose a revised direction.

### Lesson 0011 — Stakeholder alignment
Goal: manage conflicting goals between business, design, and development.
Skills: priorities, constraints, risks, compromises, decision summaries.
Evidence of mastery: mediate a short conflict between a client and a developer.

### Lesson 0012 — Product presentations and case studies
Goal: tell the story of a project clearly.
Skills: problem, context, process, decisions, outcome, reflection.
Evidence of mastery: present a case study in 3–5 minutes.

## Phase 4 — Agency and International Presence

### Lesson 0013 — Agency website positioning
Goal: define who the studio helps, what it delivers, and why it is different.
Skills: positioning statements, service descriptions, outcome-focused copy.
Evidence of mastery: draft a clear homepage hero and service section.

### Lesson 0014 — Proposals and scope documents
Goal: write proposals that reduce ambiguity and protect value.
Skills: objectives, deliverables, exclusions, timeline, revisions, payment terms.
Evidence of mastery: produce a short proposal for a real service.

### Lesson 0015 — Outreach and relationship building
Goal: start conversations with potential international clients.
Skills: personalized outreach, relevance, proof, low-pressure calls to action.
Evidence of mastery: write three tailored messages for different prospect types.

### Lesson 0016 — Subscription and retainer conversations
Goal: explain recurring design support clearly.
Skills: capacity, turnaround, request queue, boundaries, ongoing value.
Evidence of mastery: pitch the studio subscription model and answer common objections.

## Phase 5 — Listening, Thinking, and Music

### Lesson 0017 — Thinking in chunks
Goal: reduce word-by-word translation.
Skills: retrieve reusable phrases as complete units.
Evidence of mastery: respond to familiar scenarios with minimal pause.

### Lesson 0018 — Listening for meaning before detail
Goal: understand the main message without needing every word.
Skills: gist, keywords, prediction, tolerance for ambiguity.
Evidence of mastery: summarize a short work-related clip after one or two listens.

### Lesson 0019 — Connected speech and real pronunciation
Goal: recognize how familiar words change in natural speech.
Skills: reductions, linking, stress, rhythm.
Evidence of mastery: identify and repeat selected chunks from authentic audio.

### Lesson 0020 — Music as listening practice
Goal: use Beatles, Oasis, Arctic Monkeys, and similar music to improve listening and emotional vocabulary.
Skills: lyric interpretation, connected speech, imagery, reusable chunks.
Evidence of mastery: explain a verse’s meaning and reuse one chunk in a new personal context.

## Weekly rhythm

- Day 1: one guided lesson and first attempt.
- Day 2: short retrieval practice from memory.
- Day 3: real input linked to the lesson.
- Day 4: roleplay or speaking practice.
- Day 5: transfer the skill to a different context.
- Day 6: light review through music or content.
- Day 7: rest or optional immersion.

## Lesson format

Each lesson should follow this sequence:

1. Mission-linked scenario.
2. First attempt by Luis.
3. One small concept or pattern.
4. Immediate practice.
5. Feedback by layers: meaning → grammar/concept → naturalness → professional tone.
6. Transfer to a second real scenario.
7. Retrieval prompt for a future session.

## Progress rule

Do not move forward because a topic was explained. Move forward when Luis demonstrates the skill in a new scenario without copying the model answer.
